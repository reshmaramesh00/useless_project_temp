What exactly is this?

The belief this borrows from

In Hindu tradition, crows hold a particular office: during Shraddha and the fortnight of Pitru Paksha, they are treated as emissaries of the Pitrs — one's departed ancestors. Food is set out for them (kaka bali), and if a crow eats it, the offering is considered received and the ancestor, satisfied. A crow lingering near the house, in folk belief, is often read as a relative checking in.

What we did with that belief

We built a website that takes a photo of a crow and, with the seriousness of a government office and the accuracy of a fortune cookie, issues it a name, a cause of death, and a place of origin. It is not a real genealogy service. It is not a real ornithology tool. It is, technically, a form with delusions of grandeur.

Proceed only if you are prepared to learn your neighbourhood crow was, apparently, a retired schoolteacher from Palakkad.

form 7-K · photographic submission

File a photograph for review

One (1) crow per submission. Pigeons, humans, and houseplants will be noted and judged.

For best results, submit a clear, zoomed-in photograph where the crow fills most of the frame. Distant or blurry sightings confuse the review process (and, allegedly, the ancestors).
